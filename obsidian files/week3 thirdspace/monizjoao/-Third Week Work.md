
So to start off the week me and oxy researched about the lenses and attempted to create a simulation for the ray tracing of the displays through the lenses. This turned out to be a failure and very unnecessary for the modeling of the lenses themselves. Oxy directed me on how to go about modeling the lenses and I started with a simple circle to find the optical center of the displays.

![[Pasted image 20261004204713.png]]
*Optical center of displays*

*Lapse for the circle lenses before cut:* https://lapse.hackclub.com/timelapse/2Iiws-dVEZlJ

After creating the circle displays I started making the holders for them. They needed to be made in three parts and also needed a gaskets and two bumpers to squish the lenses. The gasket stays in the middle to separate the two fresnel lenses.

Then Oxy helped me cut the lens model to into the shape we wanted the lenses to have. I used the spline tool and a bunch of focal points to model it out. They turned great and the only problem is that we couldn't make sketches on the sides but that was fine since we never ended using that part so whatever.

After this I started on the cut lens holders. Using the Use tool i selected the outline of the lenses and offset it by 2mm in thickness. Then I extruded it into 2 separate parts unlike the circle ones that took 3 parts. These parts were initially 2mm in depth each but we had to thicken them up a bit so they ended up being 3mm each. Then Oxy made the shell on the lens holders and then we made holes on them for pins. These are for prototypes only however since the final project is planned that we'll just glue the 3 parts together and the pins are just to hold them temporarily.

Then it was just a matter of moving the lenses 14-15 mm in front of the displays for the fresnel lenses to give clear imaging. To do that I used the circular lens sketch to move the central point of the cut lenses into the central point of the circular ones. Then a bit of adjusting just to make the lenses fit snuggly in front of the display without any part of the lens going over the display.
